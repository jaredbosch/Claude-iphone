02Flights · Arrive by Wed 14 Apr

Three cities, one island, and the once-daily bottleneck.

Hard dates now: everyone on-island by Wednesday 14 April for the Thursday 15:00 check-in — SEA/PHX should target Tuesday 13. Book the return for Saturday 24 April; Friday checkout is the morning of the 23rd and the once-daily GND departure leaves no margin for customs, the taxi, and a delay. SEA, PHX and DFW all funnel through the same eastern/southern hubs — MIA, CLT (American), ATL (Delta), JFK/BOS (JetBlue). DFW yes · PHX marginal · SEA red-eye only

The island flight leaves the US hub mid-morning. A normal PHX–MIA lands after it's gone. Overnight at MIA/CLT converts a fragile connection into a robust two-segment plan.

Baggage — soft duffels, exactly right

If any leg uses SVG Air / interCaribbean turboprops: 44 lb free first bag, 70 lb hard cap (over → freight, space-available), carry-on ≤13 lb. Keep each duffel ≤44 lb and split weight across people.

✈ The arrival rule — with the delay math

Arrive on-island no later than the day before a Thursday check-in — Tuesday for the SEA/PHX crews. Island legs are once-daily: a missed connection isn't "the next flight in two hours," it's +24 hours, and a Wednesday-for-Thursday plan has effectively zero slack. A Tuesday arrival buys a full recovery day.

Templates — DFW: single AA ticket via MIA/CLT, arrive Wed. PHX: fly to MIA, overnight, morning island flight, arrive Tue. SEA: overnight-at-hub (preferred) or SEA–MIA red-eye (high-risk), arrive Tue. For the one-way plan, position via Barbados (BGI) on interCaribbean/LIAT and add a buffer day.

Wildcard: SVD (Argyle) as a base or joining point

St Vincent's Argyle airport drops you in the middle of the cruising ground — but it's the thinnest air link and none of the three shortlisted operators base there. It earns a place as a joining airport, not a primary base.

The trade

Closest to the Grenadines, farthest from a backup flight

SVD is essentially American via Miami (daily except Tue), plus Caribbean Airlines one-stop via POS/BGI — one broken thread and there's no same-day alternative. But it's a short hop to Bequia, Mustique and the Cays, cutting the Grenada delivery legs entirely.

How you'd actually use it

Two ways: (1) fly SVD, then SVG Air / ferry to Bequia or Union to meet a boat positioned there; or (2) switch to a St Vincent-based operator (e.g. Barefoot, Blue Lagoon) for a pure Grenadines loop — giving up the Moorings one-way and the Pitons.

Trim decoder — where A/C + watermaker actually live

The single most useful thing we learned: on the 45-ft+ Caribbean cats, generator, A/C and watermaker are equipment on the hull, not perks of a trim level. The trim tiers mostly buy hull age and Wi-Fi. Dream is the exception that needs paperwork.

Club → Exclusive → Exclusive Plus

Age tiers: Club ≈ 3–10 yrs (Wi-Fi ~$245/wk extra) · Exclusive ≈ newer, free Wi-Fi · Exclusive Plus < 1–2 yrs, documented as A/C + watermaker + generator. "Air conditioning comes on all yachts with a generator."

Ask for: Exclusive or Exclusive Plus.

◆ The cabin math for eight

Eight people is four doubles minimum — which every boat above provides — but a week aboard is a different animal with a fifth cabin: bags off the beds, a snorer quarantined, or two singles who'd rather not share. That's the honest case for the 5000/5200/Bali 4.6 over the 45-footers, and it costs roughly $500–1,300 more per person for the week.

▲ Watermaker — the one to chase in writing

A/C is easy to verify (if the boat has a generator, it has A/C). The watermaker is the least-guaranteed system in every fleet — aggregator listings routinely show A/C without one. Whatever you book, get the watermaker named on the booking confirmation for the specific hull. Eight people showering daily is exactly the load that makes it non-negotiable.

The channel legs

Bequia Channel — short, steep, wind-against-current, 1–2 m on the beam; often the roughest of the trip. Carriacou→Grenada — 33 nm, more into the trades, start on the morning breeze. Reef early; both are fine for a competent crew on a settled day.

▲ Kick 'em Jenny — live hazard, standing exclusion zone

The submarine volcano ~5 nm N of Grenada sits at Alert Yellow (its permanent baseline — it can't be seen and can erupt without warning) as of 2026; a brief seismic swarm in Aug 2025 stayed Yellow. A 1.5 km exclusion zone is always in force (5 km at higher alerts). Escaping gas can also sink a boat by cutting water density — this is a keep-clear, not just an eruption risk.

Routing: run up Grenada's calm lee coast to Sauteurs, then cross — staying west/seaward of the zone; don't cut between the volcano and Île de Ronde. Re-check the UWI-SRC / NaDMA alert level the morning you sail north and widen your offing to 5 km if it's above Yellow.
